Momiji Field Notes

momiji · 紅葉 · the autumn turning of the Japanese maples, Acer palmatum

Ten nights built for a gardener: bonsai villages and caudex dealers, moss temples and Meiji stroll gardens, three great conservatories, and the momiji — the maples of Kyoto at the moment they turn. Every stop chosen for what it teaches about plants and the shaping of ground.

The shape of the trip

Sam flies out on Wed 18 Nov and lands the next morning; the return flight leaves on Sun 29. In between: a single anti-clockwise loop with no backtracking. Tokyo first for nurseries and bonsai (colour there peaks last), Kyoto in the heart of its maple week, then over the mountains to Kanazawa for pines under fresh yukitsuri, and one last Tokyo evening among lit maples.

Field guide to a Japanese November

What's actually performing this month — and the techniques you'll see gardeners doing in real time. Colour bars are the leaf, flower or fruit you're looking for.

Plants

Japanese maple紅葉 · 楓

Acer palmatum, A. amoenum, A. buergerianum

The point of the trip. Note how many named cultivars are actually seedling-grown in temple gardens; Tōfuku-ji's “Tsūtenkyō momiji” is a trident maple that turns gold, not red.

帰

Bringing it home, and getting around

The unglamorous half: what you can legally carry, how the trains price out, and what to say at a nursery counter.

Plants and seeds — the honest position

  • Japan's side is easy. The Plant Protection Station (MAFF) has export counters at Haneda, Narita and Kansai. Present soil-free plants or seeds with a short form and they issue a free English phytosanitary certificate, usually same day.
  • The destination is the problem. UK: most live plants and seeds from non-EU countries need a phytosanitary certificate and IPAFFS pre-notification; woody genera like Acer, Pinus and Prunus carry extra rules or prohibitions. EU: certificate for essentially everything, with a high-risk list banning many woody genera. Australia: BICON permit + certificate + post-entry quarantine. Soil is prohibited almost everywhere.
  • Practical rule: seeds of herbaceous species with a certificate are usually feasible; bonsai are a specialist commercial export, not carry-on. Buy tools, pots, kanuma/akadama, books, and take photographs of the plants.

Rail — skip the national pass

  • Tokyo→Kyoto Nozomi ~¥14,170 · Kyoto→Kanazawa ~¥9,300 · Kanazawa→Tokyo ~¥14,380 = ~¥38,000, well under the ¥50,000 seven-day JR Pass (¥53,000 from 1 Oct 2026). Point-to-point wins.
  • Book on SmartEX (Tōkaidō), e5489 (JR West) and Eki-net (JR East), or all three via Klook. Nozomi isn't covered by the JR Pass anyway.
  • Welcome Suica or Mobile Suica for every metro, bus and konbini in all three cities.
  • Takkyūbin (Yamato) forwards a suitcase hotel-to-hotel overnight for ¥2,000–2,600 — hand it in by early afternoon. Ideal for heavy pots.

Pack for a gardener

  • Shoes that come off fast — you'll remove them twenty times a day at temple halls.
  • Thin gloves and a warm layer for 6 a.m. Kenroku-en; a real waterproof for Kanazawa.
  • A small notebook — many gardens ban photography indoors (Daisen-in) or in nurseries.
  • Cash: nurseries, markets and small temples are cash-only. ¥30,000 in hand.
  • Empty space in the bag for pots and tools; a hard case for scissors in hold luggage.
